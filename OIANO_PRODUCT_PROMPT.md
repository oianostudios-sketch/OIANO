# OIANO StudioOS — Product & Design Master Prompt
## "Discipline · Order · Sound"

---

## What This Is

OIANO StudioOS is a studio operating system for the music business. Not a booking tool.
Not a CRM. An operating system. The difference is that an operating system is the substrate
everything else runs on — it remembers, it connects, it gives context to every action.

The target is the triangle: **artist · producer · studio operator.** Each point of that
triangle has different pressures, different measures of success, and different emotional
relationships with the studio. The product must speak fluently to all three without
ever feeling like three products bolted together.

The design language is OIANO: the wave. Discipline brought to chaos. Order made visible.
Sound as data. Every screen should feel like the inside of a world-class recording facility —
dark, precise, nothing wasted, everything intentional.

## The Three Personas — Go Deep

### 1. The Artist

**Who they are:**
A musician building a career. Could be emerging or established. They are spending real money
to be here. Every session is both a financial decision and an act of faith — faith that the
time and cost will yield something that matters.

**What they actually need (not what they say they need):**
- To feel that the studio *knows* them. That walking back in after three weeks doesn't mean
  starting from zero. The engineer should already know they prefer rooms tracked warm.
- Their identity to exist beyond any single session. The Passport is this — a portable,
  shareable record of who they are creatively: genres, vocal type, energy, what themes
  they write about, their session history.
- Transparency without bureaucracy. They want to know what they owe, what they've spent,
  what's booked — without having to ask anyone.
- To feel like the studio is rooting for them. Not just taking their money.
- Frictionless re-booking. If they loved a session, the path back to the same room, same
  engineer, same time slot should be near-instant.

**Emotional beat that matters most:**
The moment an artist logs in and sees their history — hours spent, sessions completed,
creative DNA filling out — they should feel *established*. Not like a customer. Like a
resident of this place. The dashboard is their territory.

**What failure looks like:**
Generic. Booking confirmation emails. "Your appointment is confirmed." Cold transactional
language. A wallet balance with no personality. Any screen that could belong to a dentist's
office booking system.

**Design standards for the artist:**
- The Passport is never just a card. It is the artist's identity artefact within OIANO.
  It should feel like something worth sharing, worth framing.
- Stats are personal milestones, not analytics. "62 hours at OIANO" reads differently than
  "62 hours booked." Language matters.
- The next session countdown is not a countdown — it is anticipation. Design for that feeling.
- Wallet balance should never feel like a meter running down. Frame it as creative capital.
- Mobile first for artists. They are checking their next session from backstage, not at a desk.

---

### 2. The Producer

**Who they are:**
A producer operates differently from an artist. They may be booking sessions for clients,
managing multiple projects simultaneously, and thinking across weeks and months rather than
individual sessions. They are also potentially the bridge between the studio operator and
the artist — the one who arranges the logistics, manages creative energy, and carries
reputation on both sides.

**What they actually need:**
- Session continuity. They need to know exactly where a project was left — what was tracked,
  what engineer ran it, what notes were taken, what the next creative direction is.
- Client visibility. When they have three artists booked across different studios this week,
  they need a view that collapses all of that into one manageable picture.
- Fast re-booking for recurring patterns. If they bring the same artist every Tuesday at 2pm,
  the system should recognise that pattern and make re-booking frictionless.
- A professional front to their clients. The Passport system, when a producer shares an
  artist's creative DNA with an engineer before the session, means that engineer walks in
  prepared. This is the kind of detail that builds reputation.
- Revenue clarity. Producers often handle payment on behalf of clients. They need to see
  what's paid, what's outstanding, and what's coming up — cleanly.

**Emotional beat that matters most:**
Control without micromanagement. A producer's greatest frustration is chasing status —
"Has the session been confirmed? Did the engineer get my notes? Is the room set up right?"
OIANO should eliminate every one of those chases.

**What failure looks like:**
Anything that makes them feel like a passenger in their own workflow. Lack of session notes.
No way to communicate creative context before a booking. Generic engineer assignment with no
creative matching. Having to call the studio to check if a room is available.

---

### 3. The Studio Operator

**Who they are:**
The studio operator runs the business. They care about utilization, revenue, artist
relationships, staff, and the reputation of the facility. They are often the person who
built the studio from scratch — it is their name on the door, their investment, their vision
of what recording should feel like.

**What they actually need:**
- Total situational awareness without leaving a single screen. The Command Centre is their
  instrument. They need to know: who is in the building right now, who is next, what revenue
  has been collected today, and whether anything is about to go wrong.
- Revenue that closes itself. The worst part of running a studio is chasing payments.
  Every outstanding balance should be visible, actionable, and ideally automated.
- Artist relationship intelligence. Which artists are regular? Which ones haven't been back
  in 60 days? Which genres are trending in their bookings? This data exists — the system
  should surface it without the operator having to query anything.
- Staff and room management without scheduling software. Engineers, rooms, services — it
  should all live in OIANO, not in a separate calendar.
- The ability to intervene gracefully. If a session is running over, if a payment is missing,
  if an artist has three pending bookings with no deposit — the system should flag it and
  give the operator a path to act, not just observe.

**Emotional beat that matters most:**
Confidence. A studio operator should be able to walk into any situation — an investor call,
a conversation with an artist, a question from a new engineer — and answer from memory
because the system has given them such clear, consistent situational awareness that the
numbers, the relationships, the history live in their head.

**What failure looks like:**
Having to log into three different tools to answer one question. Data that's stale. Graphs
that don't connect to action. A "beautiful dashboard" that doesn't actually tell them
anything useful. Any screen that feels designed for a tech company rather than a studio.

## Design Philosophy

### The wave is the language

OIANO's visual identity is the waveform. Not as decoration — as meaning. The wave is a
direct representation of what the studio does: it converts creative energy into measurable
signal. Every animation in the system should reference this. Wave bars that breathe when
the studio is open, pulse when a session is live, and go still when the room goes offline
are not UI flourishes — they are the system communicating in its native tongue.

### Dark like a studio, not dark like a startup

The difference between studio dark and tech-startup dark:
- Studio dark is warm. There are candles, amber lamps, the glow of rack units.
  `#070707` bg with `#C9A84C` gold is studio dark.
- Startup dark is cold. Grey surfaces, blue accents, clinical spacing.

OIANO should feel like you could run a session in the same environment you manage one in.
The operator should feel as immersed in the space as the artist.

### Typography carries feeling

- **Playfair Display** for names, moments, headlines — the human things. An artist's name
  on their passport. A live session headline. Revenue figures. These are the things that
  matter most and they deserve the weight of a serif.
- **DM Sans** for interface copy — light, readable, never competing with the brand moments.
- **JetBrains Mono** for data — times, codes, passport numbers, counters. Precision requires
  a precision typeface.

These three voices must never be swapped. Putting a time in Playfair is wrong. Putting an
artist's name in mono is wrong.

### States are stories

Every piece of data in OIANO exists in time, and time creates states. A session is not just
"confirmed" — it is upcoming, soon, live, overrunning, or complete. A room is not just
"booked" — it could be open, incoming, back-to-back, or fully booked for the day.

Design for every state. An empty schedule is not a bug — it is a state that says
"studio is ready." Design for it intentionally. An overrunning session is not an edge case
— it is one of the most emotionally charged moments in a studio's day. Design for that tension.

### Friction is the enemy

Every click that doesn't need to exist is money a studio didn't collect, a booking an artist
didn't make, a payment that won't arrive. The measure of OIANO's quality is not how many
features it has — it is how many decisions it makes so the user doesn't have to.

## The Ecosystem Vision

OIANO is not a product. It is an ecosystem with a flywheel:

**The artist builds their Passport.** Creative DNA, session history, profile strength.
This is their identity in the music business — portable, shareable, real.

**The studio builds relationships through data.** Every booking deepens the operator's
understanding of their roster: what genres are trending, which artists are regulars, who
has been away too long, which rooms are underutilized.

**The producer connects artists to studios.** When a producer brings a new artist to OIANO,
that artist inherits the system immediately — their Passport starts building from session one.

**Intelligence compounds.** The AI summary of an artist's profile, the trending genre report,
the utilization patterns — these get smarter with every session. A studio using OIANO for
two years should feel the difference from a studio using it for two weeks.

**Revenue closes the loop.** When payment is transparent, automated, and frictionless,
studios stay financially healthy, artists stay booked, and the whole ecosystem grows.

The flywheel: more artists → more data → better intelligence → more value for operators →
better experience for artists → more artists.

## Feature Priority Framework

When evaluating any feature, ask these questions in order:

1. **Does it reduce friction for one of the three personas?**
   If yes, it belongs. If it adds steps without clear value, it doesn't.

2. **Does it make the ecosystem more valuable as it grows?**
   Features that compound are worth more than features that are linear.
   Session history is linear. AI summary of session history compounds.

3. **Does it feel like OIANO or does it feel generic?**
   A payment button that says "Pay now" is generic.
   A payment button that says "Add studio credits" is OIANO.
   Language, animation, visual treatment — all of it is identity.

4. **Would an artist, producer, or operator show this to someone?**
   The Passport is shareable. The Command Centre is something an operator would demo
   to a potential artist. Features that people want to show off are features that grow
   the ecosystem.

5. **Does it work when nothing is happening?**
   Empty states are underdesigned in most products. In OIANO, "no sessions today"
   should feel like "the studio is ready." Not like a blank screen.

## Technical Standards (non-negotiable)

- All auth through `useAuthStore()` — never touch tokens in components
- All API calls through `lib/api.ts` — never raw axios
- All env vars `VITE_*` — never `process.env.REACT_APP_*`
- All errors handled by `AppError` → `error.middleware.ts`
- All list endpoints paginated with metadata
- All request bodies validated by Zod before controller logic
- Wallet balance guard on every booking creation
- Studio scope (`slug = 'dreamz-music-lab'`) on every booking query
- Webhook route mounted before `express.json()` — always
- `SINGLE_STUDIO_MODE` assertion on startup

## What "Done" Feels Like

An artist signs up. In 10 minutes, they have a Passport with a code, a wallet, and a booking.
Their first session happens. Afterwards, their hours go up, their session count goes up,
their AI summary updates. The studio operator saw them on the Command Centre in real time.
The producer who brought them got a session confirmation without making a call.

Three weeks later, the artist opens the app backstage at a show and shares their Passport
to prove they record at Dreamz. The studio operator gets a new booking inquiry from someone
who saw that Passport.

That's the flywheel. That's what OIANO is building toward. Every line of code, every design
decision, every feature priority should be measured against whether it moves that story forward.
