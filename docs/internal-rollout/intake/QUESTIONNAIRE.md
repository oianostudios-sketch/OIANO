# How do you actually work?

We're starting to use OIANO for real work with the people we already make things
with. Before we change anything, we want to understand how *you* work, in your own
words, rather than guess from your job title.

**Pick one real piece of work you did recently** and answer about that one. A
concrete example is far more useful than a general description. If your work looks
very different from project to project, you can do this twice.

It takes about 30 minutes. Write, send voice notes, or answer in conversation —
whatever is easiest. Skip anything that doesn't apply. There are no wrong answers,
and "it depends" is a fine answer if you tell us what it depends on.

**What happens to your answers.** They are kept privately by the OIANO team and are
not published or shared with other participants. Before we build anything based on
them, we'll show you how we understood your work and ask you to confirm or correct it.
You can ask us to change or delete your answers at any time. Please don't include
passwords, private files, or other people's personal details.

---

## 1. You and the work

1. What do you call what you do?
2. What was this piece of work, in a sentence or two? Who was it for?
3. What was your part in it — and what was *not* your part?

## 2. Before you start

4. What do you need before you can begin? (For example: a brief, references, a
   script, music, a location, people, equipment, a budget, a decision from someone.)
5. Who gives you those things, and how do they usually reach you?
6. What usually goes wrong or arrives late at this stage?

## 3. Doing the work

7. Walk us through the steps, in your own words, from start to finish.
8. Where do you hand something to someone else, or wait for someone? What exactly
   gets passed along?
9. What do you need to arrange: times, places, rooms, people, equipment? How far
   ahead?
10. Who else works alongside you, and who do you need to stay in touch with?

## 4. What you deliver

11. What do you hand over at the end? (Files, a performance, a text, prints,
    something else.) Roughly how big are the files, if any?
12. Do you deliver drafts or versions along the way? What do you call them?
13. Who should be able to see each thing you deliver, and when?

## 5. Review and finishing

14. Who looks at your work and says yes or asks for changes? How do they ask?
15. How many rounds of changes are normal? When do you push back?
16. How do you know the work is *finished*? Who decides?

## 6. Credit, permission and payment

17. How do you want to be credited? Exactly what words?
18. What needs to be agreed before or during the work: fee, usage, ownership,
    where it can be shown, for how long? Who has to agree?
19. If the work shows you (your face, body, voice or name), what permission do you
    want to give, and to whom? What would you want to be able to take back?
20. How and when do you expect to be paid, if at all?

## 7. Privacy and changes

21. What about this work should stay private, and from whom?
22. If you left the work partway, or someone else did, what should happen?

## 8. What would help

23. What should a tool like OIANO remind you about?
24. What decisions should it **never** make for you?
25. What wastes your time today?
26. If one thing improved this week, what would make OIANO worth using for you?

---

Anything we didn't ask that we should have? Tell us.

**Thank you. Work creates evidence, and this is where yours starts.**
